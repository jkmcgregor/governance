# Enterprise AI Acceptable Use & Governance Framework (EAI-UGF)
**Version:** 2.4.0  
**Effective Date:** October 24, 2026  
**Regulatory Alignment:** NIST AI 100-1 (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) & EU AI Act (Risk Classifications)  
**Applies To:** All Full-Time Employees, Contractors, Third-Party Vendors, and Managed Service Providers.

---

## 1. Generative AI & LLM Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

### 1.1 Purpose & Scope
This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) governs the deployment, interaction with, and integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) and Large Language Models (LLMs) across all corporate systems, networks, and business units.

### 1.2 Core Principles
* **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL):** All AI-generated code, legal text, financial models, and strategic communications must undergo rigorous human review and validation prior to publication, execution, or external dissemination.
* **Transparency & Disclosure:** Internal documents or customer-facing materials substantially created using AI must be labeled accordingly per corporate brand and transparency guidelines.
* **Accountability:** Employees remain fully accountable for any output generated by AI tools they utilize in the scope of their employment, regardless of whether the output was modified.

## 2. Permitted vs. Prohibited Data Classifications

To protect proprietary intellectual property (IP), personally identifiable information (PII), and regulatory compliance boundaries, data interaction with AI is strictly categorized into three tiers:

| Data Classification | Description & Examples | Permitted AI Handling | Prohibited Actions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1: Public / Open** | Marketing collateral, public press releases, open-source software libraries, non-confidential documentation. | Fully permitted on enterprise-approved and consumer-tier public AI tools. | Uploading internal metrics or unreleased announcements. |
| **Tier 2: Internal / Confidential** | Internal organizational charts, general IT policies, high-level project roadmaps, non-sensitive business memos. | Permitted **only** on enterprise-managed AI platforms with Zero Data Retention (ZDR) agreements and enterprise licensing. | Inputting into consumer-grade, ad-supported free tiers of LLMs (e.g., standard consumer ChatGPT, free Claude). |
| **Tier 3: Restricted / PII / IP** | Source code, proprietary algorithms, customer PII, financial records, PHI, M&A data, trade secrets. | Strictly prohibited on all external cloud LLMs unless processed via an air-gapped, isolated, or dedicated enterprise tenant with explicit InfoSec sign-off. | Any input into public tools, fine-tuning public models with corporate codebases without legal review. |

## 3. Third-Party AI Vendor Risk Evaluation Checklist

Before any business unit can onboard, license, or integrate a third-party AI product or API, the vendor must clear the following security and compliance evaluation managed by the Office of the CISO and AI Governance Board:

* [ ] **Data Privacy & Training Rights:** Does the vendor explicitly state in their Data Processing Agreement (DPA) that enterprise data will **not** be used to train baseline or third-party models?
* [ ] **Compliance Certification:** Does the vendor hold current SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 42001 (AI Management System), and ISO 27001 certifications?
* [ ] **EU AI Act Classification Check:** If operating within or serving EU markets, has the vendor classified their system (Unacceptable, High, Limited, Minimal) and provided technical documentation conforming to Annex IV of the EU AI Act?
* [ ] **Model Explainability & Bias Testing:** Has the vendor supplied documentation regarding training data provenance, mitigation of algorithmic bias, and procedures for red-teaming?
* [ ] **Data Residency & Sovereignty:** Are customer prompts and completions stored and processed within approved geographic boundaries (e.g., EU-US Data Privacy Framework or localized regional servers)?
* [ ] **Incident Response & Vulnerability Disclosure:** Does the vendor maintain a 24/7 security incident response process with a commitment to notify enterprise customers of model security breaches within 48 hours?

## 4. Shadow AI Prevention & API Token Governance Guidelines

### 4.1 Shadow AI Definition
"Shadow AI" refers to the unauthorized adoption of unvetted AI applications, browser extensions, or SaaS utilities by employees without formal Information Security approval.

### 4.2 Prevention & Enforcement
* **Network Perimeter Controls:** Corporate firewalls, Secure Web Gateways (SWG), and DNS filters actively block known unauthorized consumer AI endpoints and wrapper applications.
* **API Token Lifecycle Management:** All API keys or developer tokens connected to foundational model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Google Vertex) must be provisioned via the centralized Developer Portal managed by Cloud Engineering. Hardcoding API keys in public or unencrypted repositories is an immediate violation of InfoSec policy.
* **Browser Extension Restrictions:** Enterprise-managed browsers (Chrome/Edge Enterprise) enforce policies blocking unauthorized AI writing assistants and web scrapers.
